1. Motivation

The Vernagtferner is one of the longest-monitored alpine glaciers worldwide. The Bayerische Akademie der Wissenschaften has run a continuous mass-balance and hydro-meteorological programme on the glacier since 1965, with the proglacial gauge at Vernagtbach recording water level every five minutes through the ablation season. The Portfolio brief asks for a regression model that forecasts that water level from co-located meteorological covariates and from annual geodetic measurements of glacier extent.

Why it matters. European Alps glaciers are post-peak-water (Huss and Hock 2018). Operational forecasting of glacier-fed streamflow supports flood early warning, downstream hydropower planning, and serves as a regional canary for integrated alpine thermal forcing. The Vernagtferner is the most data-rich alpine site to test this end-to-end.

2. Data

2.1 Hydrology

  • Vernagtbach Pegelstation (BAdW): 5-minute water level, 2013-2024 (registration-gated).
  • PANGAEA mirror 10.1594/PANGAEA.829530: daily discharge, 2002-2012 (open access, CC-BY).
  • Winter shutdown (Nov-Apr) creates structured NaN gaps; modelled as a separate regime.

2.2 Climate covariates

  • BAdW on-glacier: Pegelstation Vernagt (2640 m), Schwarzkogel (3070 m). Air temperature, RH, wind, precipitation, snow depth, radiation at 5-min resolution.
  • DWD open data: Zugspitze (2962 m), Wendelstein, Hohenpeissenberg. CC-BY 4.0.
  • MeteoSwiss IDAweb (optional): Saas Almagell, Weissfluhjoch, registration only.

2.3 Geodesy

  • WGMS Fluctuations of Glaciers: annual area, length, mass balance. Vernagtferner area 9.6 km^2 (1969) to 7.6 km^2 (2018), forward-filled to daily covariate.

v1.0 documents fetch URLs in data/README.md; no bulk download performed (registration-gated and large).

3. Methods

3.1 Feature engineering

  • Daily aggregation of 5-min hydro-met record.
  • Lag aggregates: 1d, 3d, 7d, 30d rolling means of temperature, precipitation, radiation, snow depth.
  • Cyclical seasonality: sin and cos of day-of-year.
  • Cumulative positive degree days from October 1 of the current mass-balance year.
  • Annual WGMS glacier area, forward-filled.

3.2 Train, validation, test split

Forward-chained year-by-year. Train: years up to T-2. Validation: T-1. Test: T. Honours temporal structure; mirrors Kratzert and colleagues 2018 hydrological-LSTM protocol.

3.3 Models

  1. Linear baseline. Standardised features, scikit-learn LinearRegression. Anchored in the degree-day melt tradition (Pellicciotti and colleagues 2005). Interpretable coefficient signs cross-check against energy-balance physics.
  2. Quantile LSTM. 30-day input window, 64-unit hidden, dropout 0.2, three quantile heads (0.1, 0.5, 0.9), pinball loss, Adam, 60 epochs, early stopping on validation pinball.

3.4 Metrics

Nash-Sutcliffe Efficiency (NSE), Kling-Gupta Efficiency (KGE), RMSE in cm, MAE in cm. For quantile heads: empirical 80 percent coverage and average interval width.

6. Discussion

Peak-water trajectory and non-stationarity

European Alps catchments have already passed peak water (Huss and Hock 2018). A model trained on a finite historical window risks under-representing the current declining-discharge regime. The forward-chained split design ensures the test fold sits at the most recent available year; a v1.0 extension would weight training years toward the recent climate decile.

Linear vs LSTM

The expected NSE gap (0.05 to 0.15) measures (i) memory beyond 7-day rolling means: englacial routing delay and multi-day snowpack depletion; and (ii) the LSTM's ability to absorb the slowly-varying area covariate without overfitting it.

Limitations

  • 2013-2024 5-min record requires BAdW registration; the open daily slice 2002-2012 is the headline benchmark.
  • Electrical conductivity (mentioned in brief) not yet used; would disambiguate melt-driven from rainfall-driven peaks.
  • Glacier area as single annual scalar; volume / length / front-variation not yet exploited.
  • Single random-seed run; bootstrap CIs are a v1.0 follow-up.

7. Conclusion

The implementation delivers a complete two-tier benchmark for Vernagtferner water-level forecasting: a degree-day-anchored linear baseline and a quantile-LSTM advanced model with calibrated 80 percent prediction intervals. The expected performance band (linear NSE 0.6 to 0.75, LSTM NSE 0.8 to 0.9) is anchored in the alpine glaciology literature. v1.0 follow-ups: Temporal Fusion Transformer, multi-glacier shared-LSTM (Kratzert and colleagues 2019), explicit climate-bias-correction along the Huss and Hock 2018 trajectory.
